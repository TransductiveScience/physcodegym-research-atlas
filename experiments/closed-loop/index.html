Research proposal / Beyond simulation

A laboratory that makes theories disagree

Convert reconstruction anomalies into automated, experimentally discriminating tests.

The leap PhysCodeGym does not make

PhysCodeGym inspects the physics of programs generated from videos. To search for new physical effects, the system must connect competing models to physical apparatus, sensors, calibration, and repeatable measurements. A simulated conservation failure is a diagnostic clue about software; only real measurements can establish a laboratory discrepancy.

A practical architecture

AObserve

Record synchronized high-speed video plus calibrated position, force, time or optical sensors. Store raw observations and sensor uncertainty.

BGenerate

Fit several alternative physical models and keep their assumptions, priors and parameters explicitly inspectable.

CDiscriminate

Choose a safe intervention that maximizes expected disagreement among predictions.

DRun & falsify

Apply the intervention, collect blinded measurements, repeat controls and update or eliminate models.

Three low-barrier experiment candidates

  1. Pendulum parameter identification: distinguish linear damping, quadratic drag and pivot friction from optical motion traces; vary amplitude and bob geometry.
  2. Rolling contact and slip: compare spin, velocity and material-specific contact models from synchronized camera data; introduce controlled surface changes.
  3. Driven mechanical resonance: identify nonlinearity, hysteresis and frequency-response changes with a microphone or accelerometer and a programmable drive.

These are proposed experimental designs, not new findings. They offer measurable failure surfaces where an AI can propose and verify discriminating interventions with inexpensive hardware.

Evidence contract

Keep raw data, units, calibration records, null controls, code revisions, uncertainty intervals, failed runs and measured counterexamples. Reject post hoc changes to scoring rules. Preserve an audit trail from observation through theory and experiment.
