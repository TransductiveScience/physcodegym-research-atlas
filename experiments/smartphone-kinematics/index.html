<section><h2>From synthetic failures to measured phenomena</h2><p>PhysCodeGym exposes invalid code-generated worlds. To discover physical discrepancies in reality, substitute a real apparatus, a calibrated smartphone camera, repeatable interventions and an independent measurement channel. The goal is not to make a video look unusual. The goal is to force different proposed mechanisms to make observably different predictions.</p></section> <section><h2>Protocol A · Rolling dynamics</h2><p><strong>Apparatus:</strong> a track or tray, two visibly distinguishable rolling objects, high-contrast position marks, a stable phone, a ruler in the same plane, and a controlled release.</p><ol><li>Fix the camera and record a calibration grid or ruler in the experiment plane.</li><li>Film multiple runs from varied release heights; where supported, lock exposure, focus and frame timing.</li><li>Track translational centre and, using marked surface features, rotational angle.</li><li>Compare pure rolling, kinetic slipping and speed-dependent drag models.</li><li>Use a held-out surface or incline to test the models' distinct predictions.</li></ol><p><strong>Confounders:</strong> lens distortion, parallax, rolling shutter, auto-exposure, uncertain release motion and contact-dependent friction.</p></section> <section><h2>Protocol B · Pendulum damping</h2><p><strong>Apparatus:</strong> suspended bob, fixed pivot, visible scale, smartphone tripod and controlled release. Film at multiple starting angles using the same camera pose and measure successive peak amplitudes.</p><p>Fit competing differential equations, such as <code>θ″ + 2βθ′ + (g/L)sin θ = 0</code> versus a model adding a quadratic speed term. Do not treat a better in-sample fit as confirmation. Predict a new amplitude and a new bob configuration before filming.</p><p><strong>Confounders:</strong> pivot friction, rope flexibility, air movement, two-plane motion, imperfect measurement of L and shutter timing.</p></section> <section><h2>Protocol C · Resonance and hysteresis</h2><p>Record a weakly driven mechanical oscillator with a smartphone microphone or accelerometer and a programmable, limited-amplitude drive. Repeat frequency sweeps in both directions. Compare linear response against a nonlinear model; check whether any hysteresis survives changes in drive level and sensor calibration.</p><p>Keep excitation within safe limits, avoid operating machinery and document all raw signals. A microphone cannot directly provide force without a calibrated transfer function.</p></section> <section><h2>Pre-registration and evidence capture</h2><p>For every hypothesis, publish <strong>predicted curves, unit conversions, held-out conditions, uncertainty, null controls and all runs including failures</strong>. Record the original media files, frame timestamps, calibration images and immutable hashes. If the models disagree only below sensor noise, redesign the experiment rather than proclaiming an anomaly.</p><p>These three procedures are new <em>proposals</em>, not experiments already executed or anomalies already measured.</p><a class="readmore" href="/analysis/identifiability/">The mathematical reason to intervene →</a></section>
