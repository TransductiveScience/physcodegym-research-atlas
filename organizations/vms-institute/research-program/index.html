Applied research program

The institute’s 31-branch map, eight domains and the differing maturity of published, experimental and exploratory work.

Program map / September 2026 revision

31 branches across eight areas

The institute’s Applied Sciences Research describes a proposed program spanning Materials & Condensed Matter, Energy & Thermal Systems, Electronics & Information, Imaging & Diagnostics, Particles & Charge, Light & Optics, Cosmology, and Foundations & Metrology. It reports 31 mapped branches and says most remain at the derivation stage.

The program separates “foundation published,” “experiment published,” “lattice tables published,” “in development” and “exploratory.” These are internal status labels, not external certifications of correctness or readiness for industrial use.

Method declared by the institute

A single action scale and explicit anchors

The stated research discipline is to use a single action scale set at the electron and avoid refitting it across disciplines, with electron, hydrogen and muon as declared anchors. The institute proposes ratio-first predictions, uncertainty reporting and pass/fail thresholds registered before comparisons. The distinction between a used anchor and an independent test is central to documenting any actual predictive progress.

Study paths

Research branches to follow

Materials: lattice phase geometry, shared closures, surfaces and defects. Optics: diffraction dynamics, caustics, spectral convergence and interference networks. Cosmology: geometric lensing, proposed geometric residue, cosmic background and structure formation. Metrology: resonant closure, mass-channel dynamics and relativistic energy exchange.
