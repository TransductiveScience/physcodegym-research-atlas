Published bench / optical apparatus

Mass-dependent optical behavior

The institute presents a LEGO-built optical bench with a fixed slit and a narrow gap between metal blocks. It interprets reported changes in the central optical lobe as a mass-related geometric effect, and describes 1–2 mm channel spacings and a metal-density ordering. The experiment page provides a report, results data, lab notes and videos.

The applied-sciences program explicitly identifies independent replication as a first deliverable and says it has not yet occurred. Its proposed test fixes camera exposure and gain, archives raw frames, and checks whether the reported ordering persists across metals. This page reports the institute's stated findings, not an independently confirmed tabletop gravitational effect.

Exploratory analysis / astronomy

Galaxy rotation curves and geometric residue

Another publication applies a geometric-residue model to galaxy rotation curves from the SPARC data set. The institute says the exploratory fits use two parameters per galaxy and identifies the Tully–Fisher scaling, and deriving its parameters from observable baryonic mass, as unresolved issues. The page should therefore be read as a model-comparison exercise rather than confirmation of a cosmological mechanism.

What evidence separates the instruments from the interpretation?

Evidence layerQuestion to document
Raw camera dataAre unprocessed frames, camera settings, geometry and timestamps independently inspectable?
ControlsDoes the effect persist with fixed optical conditions, alternate coatings and metal/control substitutions?
Quantitative modelDoes the theory predict the sign and magnitude of the effect before looking at the outcome?
Independent repeatCan another group reproduce the stated ordering without access to fitted parameters?

These are proposed evidence checks for subsequent reporting, not claims that the original experiment necessarily omitted them.
