Independent research directory / VMS Institute

Organization, funding & reach

What the institute says about its identity, sponsorship, reviewer engagement, grants and readership, with provenance levels.

Public identity

Independent enterprise, not a university

VMS Institute describes itself as VMS Institute, LLC, Atlanta, Georgia and says it is a for-profit entity. Its website is a research hub featuring theoretical works, proposed experiments, independent-review invitations, and the AI Loader intellectual-property project. This page does not claim to have retrieved an up-to-date Georgia business registry extract, tax return or formal ownership record.

The institute also maintains an Institute section and a contact directory that separate research, support, education, investor and legal inquiries.

Funding and partnerships

Sponsorship rather than donations

The Grants & Research page states that VMS does not accept donations and that direct contributions are not tax deductible. It describes sponsor-funded replication kits for eligible researchers and educators. It also says the institute facilitates research grants among independent researchers, institutions and sponsors, without acting as the payer or grantor.

These are published operational descriptions, not proof of any particular completed grant, shipped kit or independent sponsor commitment.

Self-reported reach

Audience is not peer review

The institute claims more than 700,000 views of an experiment demonstration, more than 10,000 viewing hours, and more than one million pages served across its documents. It attributes these totals to its own analytics. These are self-reported website/video metrics; underlying analytics exports were not independently inspected for this dossier. Readership and independent scientific validation should not be represented as interchangeable.

Reviewer engagement

A public review log

The institute publishes a public-review log with selected critical exchanges and responses. The log should be understood as the organization's own account of those interactions. Quotes about third parties and characterization of their reasoning require independent verification before being treated as biographical findings or independent peer-review outcomes.
