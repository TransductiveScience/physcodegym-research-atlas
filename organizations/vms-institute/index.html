01 / Identity & public record

What is VMS Institute?

VMS Institute presents itself as a research and publishing enterprise organized around the Void–Mass–Structure (VMS) framework, a proposed geometric account of matter, fields and physical interactions. Its own site identifies the entity as VMS Institute, LLC, Atlanta, Georgia, and describes it as for-profit, not a tax-deductible charity. Those are published organizational descriptions, not an independently obtained state-registration extract.

The organization combines theoretical publications, computational verification material, proposed or reported experiments, intellectual-property filings, an AI-oriented framework loader, sponsorships and a public falsification challenge. These activities differ in evidentiary status and should be read separately.

02 / Research program

The proposed physical picture

The founding postulate describes a localized “void” as an absence in spacetime whose moving boundary has geometric action. The framework proposes that propagating voids carry energy, and that closed or trapped paths yield mass. It then extends the geometric picture to gravity, electromagnetism, particle mechanics and thermodynamics. Its background narrative also invokes non-uniformities inherited from a hypothetical earlier massless cosmological event.

These are claims within the proposed theory, not established observations. The institute publishes both approachable illustrated explanations and mathematical appendices intended to specify its deductions.

03 / Published materials

Documents, code and numerical work

The theory library is divided into front matter, foundation, mechanics, electromagnetism, thermodynamics, particle mechanics and exploratory branches, with PDF and sometimes LaTeX or reader formats. The Mathematical Bridge and its Math Appendix are especially relevant to claims of deduction from primitive assumptions.

The institute maintains a SymPy audit page. Its published statement carefully limits the audit's purpose to mathematical consistency given assumed forms, rather than proof that the assumptions describe nature. A separate collection of 150 audit sheets divides numerical exercises among mechanics, electromagnetism, particle mechanics and thermodynamics. Passing a programmatic test and testing a physical prediction are different operations.

An applied-sciences directory maps 31 research branches across eight program areas; the site describes most as still being at the derivation stage as of September 2026. Its experimental program includes a proposed mass-dependent optical measurement and an exploratory analysis of galaxy rotation curves using SPARC data.

04 / People & research responsibility

People associated with the work

Virgil Waters publicly discusses the VMS framework and shares the institute's research materials. His specific formal office and the institute's complete membership or corporate roster have not been independently established for this dossier. This directory distinguishes public authorship and advocacy from unverified legal titles.

05 / Challenge & assessment

The published $50,000 falsification challenge

The challenge page advertises a $50,000 bounty for either a mathematical break in a published derivation or a numbered prediction that fails against cited measurements. It requests exact derivation locations and demonstrations rather than objections based only on consensus. The site explicitly labels the criteria draft, pending technical-panel ratification.

Independent funding security, escrow, complete eligibility conditions and adjudication arrangements have not been verified in this dossier. A research submission prepared by Transductive Science is under internal mathematical review; no panel decision or prize award is claimed. Full unpublished challenge evidence is not reproduced here.

06 / Intellectual property

Patents, applications and licensing

The institute's patent page reproduces filing information for U.S. application 19/728,053, concerning an Artificial Intelligence Loader, filed July 1, 2026, and describes a separate physical-model calibration application 19/802,308 filed September 9, 2026, with a related international application. The site offers licensing for the AI-loader patent family. A patent application is not a granted patent; these details are the institute's published filing account unless separately corroborated in an official register.

Its licensing page distinguishes the publicly released CC BY 4.0 loader edition from patent rights it says are subject to licensing.
