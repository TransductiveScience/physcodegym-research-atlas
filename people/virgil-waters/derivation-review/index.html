Code-to-claim examination / 08 November 2026

What a passing audit actually checks

The institute's downloadable SymPy ZIP contains 32 numbered verification modules. Its distributed JSON result says every block passed on 4 March 2026. That result can be correct as a statement about the program's assertions without establishing that all the theoretical source-text conclusions were covered by those programs.

In a source-only examination of the 32 CAS_F####_VERIFY.py modules, I found no explicit implementation of the Section 7 proposed transport current or the Section 15 source-balance equation, and no Hodge-star / exterior-wedge calculus checking either statement. This is a coverage finding, not a claim that every included assertion is wrong.

For example, module F0001 begins with the textbook Lagrangian L=½mv²−V(x); module F0002 begins with a Poisson-potential formulation; and F0005 defines the electromagnetic tensor from a gauge potential and checks its action-based identities. Those are valid subjects for symbolic tests, but taking a known action or field equation as an input is distinct from deriving it exclusively from VMS's void assumptions.

Unresolved dependency

A passing algebra check can coexist with an untested implication

The formalism's proposition chain passes through the original Mathematical Bridge sections. To claim full derivational coverage, every asserted arrow from primitive assumptions to conservation equations, then to transport energy and observed scaling, needs a corresponding formal proof obligation or independent mathematical check.

The current ZIP's audit inventory and the appendix's equation map are not yet one-to-one.
