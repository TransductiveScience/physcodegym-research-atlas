<section><h2>What has actually been observed?</h2><p>The paper reports quantitative defects in executable Blender scenes produced by coding agents. Those numbers are measurements of <em>submitted simulations</em>. They are not evidence that physical laws failed in a real experiment. The published case studies are illustrative selections, not a measured prevalence rate across all 156 tasks.</p><div class="note"><b>A testable split</b><p>There are two different problems: an AI generates a physically invalid world, or the world itself behaves differently from a physical theory. PhysCodeGym addresses the first. They become tools for discovering <em>new physics</em> only after the same disagreement survives a controlled experiment with independently measured real-world states. A reconstructed world should be asked to predict what happens <em>after an intervention it has not seen</em>.</p><a class="readmore" href="/analysis/identifiability/">Why one observed trajectory cannot determine a law →</a></section>
