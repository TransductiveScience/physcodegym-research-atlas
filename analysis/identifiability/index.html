<section><h2>The inverse problem</h2><p>Suppose a camera measures projected coordinates <code>y(t) = P(x(t))</code>, where <code>x(t)</code> is an unobserved three-dimensional state and <code>P</code> is a projection with occlusion and sampling. We seek a physical model <code>dx/dt = f(x,u; θ)</code> that reproduces the observed series. Fitting the observations does not uniquely determine <code>f</code> or its parameters <code>θ</code>.</p><p>Two different mechanisms may generate the same recorded trajectory under one setting of the controls <code>u</code>. One program might physically simulate a bouncing ball; another might simply prescribe its position frame by frame. Their videos coincide, but their responses to a shifted release diverge.</p></section> <section><h2>The experiment as a separator</h2><p>Write two candidate predictions <code>y₁(t | u)</code> and <code>y₂(t | u)</code>. Instead of collecting more frames of the same trajectory, choose a safe intervention <code>u*</code> that makes their predictions differ by more than the sensor's uncertainty.</p><div class="note"><b>Illustrative design criterion</b><p><code>u* = argmaxᵤ Σₜ [ y₁(t | u) − y₂(t | u) ]² / σ²(t)</code></p><p>This is a simple disagreement-over-noise objective, not a statistical guarantee. A full design should account for correlated errors, nuisance parameters, interventions' safety and model misspecification.</p></div></section> <section><h2>Examples</h2><ol><li><strong>Table support:</strong> shift a rolling object past the table edge. A finite-table model predicts loss of support; an infinite hidden plane does not.</li><li><strong>Contact ownership:</strong> move a piece into a target obstacle. Real contact should change its trajectory; a missing collision body leaves the motion unchanged.</li><li><strong>Damping:</strong> repeat a pendulum oscillation with different amplitudes. Linear, quadratic and dry-friction models generally predict different amplitude-decay patterns.</li><li><strong>Coupling:</strong> alter a hinge, anchor or spring; models with physically active constraints respond, while keyframed motion may continue unaffected.</li></ol></section> <section><h2>Epistemic consequence</h2><p>A good fit to one video warrants the limited conclusion that a model can <em>reproduce those observations</em>. Predictive generalization under deliberately chosen interventions supports a stronger claim of mechanism adequacy. It still does not prove uniqueness or eliminate every untested alternative.</p><a class="readmore" href="/experiments/smartphone-kinematics/">Turn this principle into low-cost measured experiments →</a></section>
