<section><h2>The intervention is the measurement instrument</h2><p>Rather than asking an agent to produce prettier pixels, reconstruct a physically meaningful alternative trial while retaining the same authored scene. A change in initial position, support or constraint reveals whether the program actually contains the mechanism it claims.</p></section> <section><h2>01 · Move the wooden piece into a dish</h2><p>A GPT-6-Astra reconstruction is challenged with an altered release: the wooden piece begins 32.25 mm clear of the dish, then passes through the actual three-dimensional dish material for 158 frames, reaching a reported maximum interior depth of 4.619 mm. Removing the dish leaves the reported centre-of-mass trajectory unchanged.</p><p>What this discriminates: collision support from an independently simulated surface versus movement unaffected by that surface. <a href="/cases/newgap_wooden_piece_rolls/">Reference case metadata →</a></p></section> <section><h2>02 · Drive a billiard ball beyond the table</h2><p>The cue ball envelope clears the support boundary at y = 1.100 m starting at frame 117. It stays suspended at about 28.575 mm height for 124 samples. The author's infinite-support control reproduces the anomalous path; restricting support to the finite tabletop preserves the pre-edge prefix, then the ball falls.</p><p>What this discriminates: physically finite support versus an unbounded hidden support plane. <a href="/cases/newgap_billiards_cue_strike/">Reference billiards task →</a></p></section> <section><h2>03 · Disconnect the rotor’s base hinge</h2><p>DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (max effort) is tested by removing the sole base-to-beam hinge after the original setup. The intervention preserves the original trajectory through frame 120, then produces a table intersection across frames 123–126 and a body below the table bottom at frame 127. The intended test concerns actual contact following release, not merely whether the hinge object was declared.</p></section> <section><h2>04 · Roll toward the chess knight</h2><p>Two altered releases place a rolling object inside a nearby closed knight assembly for 46 and 29 frames. The observed maximum inward vertex depths are 0.295 and 0.105 scene units, respectively. The implicated knight meshes have no active collision owner in the submitted scene. These are two reruns of a model, not laboratory trials.</p></section> <section><h2>05 · Send the piece through a wooden token</h2><p>A closed cylindrical obstacle lacking a physical contact owner is encountered on frames 114–145 and 118–149 in two branches (32 consecutive frames each). The minimum signed distance approaches −0.110 scene units. Removing the token does not change the sampled 240-frame trajectory in the indicated branch.</p></section> <section><h2>How to strengthen each result</h2><ol><li>Pin the original program and video hashes.</li><li>Preserve the original setup and an independently physical control.</li><li>Define the intervention before evaluating outcomes.</li><li>Measure signed distances, constraint violations or a complete energy budget instead of trusting an image.</li><li>Repeat at multiple solver substep settings and document failure caps, uncertainties and numerical artifacts.</li></ol><p>These descriptions paraphrase the paper's Figures 22–26. They are examples; the paper does not report a statistically representative failure frequency from these selected scenes.</p></section>
