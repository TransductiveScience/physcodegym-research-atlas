Anomaly 01 / Energy budget

The cradle that gained energy

A simulated Newton’s cradle exhibits more gravitational potential energy than its estimated initial total energy.

Reported numerical witness

9.44 mJmeasured maximum gravitational potential energy4.97 mJestimated initial total energy+4.47 mJexcess relative to that estimate

In the GPT-6-Astra reconstruction, gravitational potential energy alone reaches 9.44 mJ after the gripper releases the balls. The benchmark reports an initial total-energy estimate of 4.97 mJ and identifies frame 236 as the measured witness. That is about 1.90× the initial estimated energy.

The rendered scene earns 1.0 on both object and event checks. Visual plausibility has concealed an energetically inconsistent implementation.

What does the measurement establish?

It demonstrates a mismatch between the reconstructed program’s passive energy budget and the expected mechanics. It does not establish that real Newton’s cradles create energy. Possible model-level causes include incorrect constraints, impulses, externally driven motion, collision settings, or inconsistent force accounting. Those are hypotheses until the specific implementation is inspected.

Interpretation boundary

“Potential exceeds estimated initial total” is the reported test. To independently validate a conservation-law claim, inspect the complete energy ledger, external work, solver artifacts, units, frame timestamps and initial-state estimate.

Replication path

  1. Retrieve the exact candidate Blender scene and task rubric.
  2. Measure kinetic, gravitational potential and any active-field work at every frame.
  3. Compare the submitted code with a passive, controlled baseline.
  4. Change solver steps and collision tolerances; record whether the energy excess persists.
