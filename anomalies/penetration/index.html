Anomaly 02 / Contact

A gripper inside solid material

Native scene coordinates expose a solid-object intersection that the rendered video can obscure.

46.3 mm

For a DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash reconstruction, the centre of the right gripper finger lies inside the blue block during frames 95–100. Its greatest reported distance from the nearest block face is 46.3 mm at frame 99.

The researchers use native block-local geometry and a slowed replay rather than relying on appearance alone. This distinction matters: occlusion in a video can hide a contact error that 3D coordinates expose.

What is anomalous?

The simulation represents a supposedly solid object penetrating another supposedly solid object. Such an event is invalid under the intended rigid-contact model, but it is a failure of the generated scene, not evidence that real matter has become permeable.

Possible causes worth testing are incorrect collision shapes, collision masks, solver configuration, kinematic overrides, or contact updates. These are diagnostic alternatives, not conclusions about this exact file.

A stronger verification

Measure signed-distance fields or nearest-surface penetration depth for every simulated frame. Compare render geometry against collision geometry. Repeat the grasp under a changed starting angle: does the violation persist, disappear, or migrate?
