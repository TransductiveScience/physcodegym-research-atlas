Anomaly 03 / Hidden contact

Invisible geometry carrying the load

A believable image hides a contact surface whose shape exceeds the visible object.

2.59–2.89 mm

The Claude Sonnet 5 ball reconstruction reportedly maintains a 2.59–2.89 mm sampled visible-material gap between ball and support after settling, yet an oversized collision sphere still provides support and traction. The ball can continue rotating because collision geometry and rendered geometry tell different stories.

In a separate billiards case, a reconstructed cue ball continues to float after being redirected beyond the finite tabletop. The original trajectory did not expose the incorrectly extended support.

Two worlds occupy the same scene

Rendering geometry determines what the viewer sees. Collision geometry determines what the physics engine feels. When these diverge, a visually persuasive video can coexist with an impossible contact mechanism.

Why this matters beyond games

In robotics, digital twins and autonomous manipulation, a controller that relies on invisible support or inaccurate collision hulls can fail when transferred to real hardware.

Diagnostic intervention

Visualize every collision hull, compute the minimum signed distance between physical surfaces, then push the object into regions excluded from the visible support geometry. The expected finite-table control falls; the anomalous candidate remains supported.
