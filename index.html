Field note 001 / 08 November 2026

When simulations break physics

Measured anomalies in AI-reconstructed worlds, and what they do (and do not) reveal about the physical universe.

01 / CLAIM

Yes, the anomalies were measured. No, they were not laboratory violations of physics.

PhysCodeGym reports numerical defects inside executable Blender reconstructions written by AI agents. The numbers are real measurements of those generated scenes. They are not measurements of a new force, an experimental breakdown of energy conservation, or an anomalous real-world material.

The benchmark

156 tasks, four physical domains

Agents observe a reference video, write an executable Blender scene, simulate it, revise the code, then submit it to visual, event, motion and physical-validity checks. Of these, 43 tasks include counterfactual interventions that test how the world behaves when conditions change.

Follow the inquiry

From an anomaly to an experiment

A visually accurate reconstruction is not proof that its hidden physical model is correct. The constructive extension is to use competing models to design discriminating real experiments, with calibrated sensors and closed-loop control.
